Firm, Medium-Firm or Extra Firm: Which Mattress for Back Pain?

What the best-known trial actually found, what “orthopedic” does and doesn’t mean, and how to choose a mattress in Calgary or Edmonton.

What the Research Actually Found

The best-known study on this question is a randomized, double-blind trial published in The Lancet in 2003. It followed 313 adults with chronic non-specific low-back pain, who were given either a firm or a medium-firm mattress, both rated on the European standard firmness scale. The researchers began from the widespread belief that a firm mattress helps low-back pain and noted that evidence for it was lacking.

After 90 days, the medium-firm group did better on pain while lying in bed and on pain-related disability, with roughly twice the odds of improvement on each. Pain on rising pointed in the same direction, but that result was less certain.

🔎 What this does and doesn’t tell you: it compared firm with medium-firm in people with chronic, non-specific low-back pain. It did not test extra firm, it is one trial, and averages hide individual differences. Read it as “firmer isn’t automatically better,” not as “medium-firm treats back pain.”

Source: Kovacs FM et al., The Lancet 2003;362:1599–1604 — PubMed abstract.

General tendencies, not medical advice. There is no industry-wide standard for firmness labels, so one brand’s “firm” can feel like another’s “extra firm.”

What “Orthopedic” and “Extra Firm” Mean (and Don’t)

“Orthopedic” on a mattress is a marketing description, not a certified medical category. There is no certification a mattress must pass to use the word, so it usually just signals a firmer, more supportive feel. “Extra firm” is likewise a brand’s own label. Because labels vary, judge a mattress by its construction and how it feels, not by the word on the box.

How to Choose Without a Showroom Visit

  1. Notice what actually bothers you: sinking into a soft surface, or pressure aches on a hard one. That tells you which direction to move.
  2. Think about your usual position and weight. Heavier sleepers sink further into any mattress, and side sleepers often want more give at the shoulders and hips.
  3. Don’t equate thick with firm. A thick mattress can feel soft, and a slim one can feel hard.
  4. Give it time. Many people need a couple of weeks to judge a new mattress fairly.
  5. If back pain is ongoing, talk to a doctor or physiotherapist first. A mattress can affect comfort, but it is not a treatment.
