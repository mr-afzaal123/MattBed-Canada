The Short Answer

There is no single winner. Memory foam and orthopedic mattresses solve different problems — and the right choice depends on how you sleep, whether you have back pain, and whether you prefer a soft or firm feel.

Here is the quick version:

  • Choose memory foam if you sleep on your side, share a bed with a partner, or want pressure relief on hips and shoulders
  • Choose orthopedic if you sleep on your back or stomach, or simply prefer a hard firm surface

Memory Foam Mattress — What it Is and Who Needs It

Memory foam was originally developed by NASA and works by responding to your body heat and weight — it softens where you press and cradles your body shape. Many people find this more comfortable at the hips, shoulders and knees, areas that can feel pressure on a hard mattress.

Memory foam is best for:

  • Side sleepers — your hip and shoulder need cushioning when you sleep on your side. Many side sleepers find memory foam comfortable here
  • Couples — when one partner moves, memory foam absorbs the movement so the other partner doesn't feel it
  • People who like a contouring feel — knees, hips and shoulders sink in slightly on memory foam
  • People moving from a soft bed — memory foam feels most similar to what most Canadians grew up sleeping on

Memory foam is not ideal for:

  • Back sleepers who like firm support
  • People who sleep hot — memory foam retains body heat more than firm foam
  • Families who prefer the hard sleeping surface common in South Asia

Orthopedic Mattress — What it Is and Who Needs It

The word "orthopedic" is not a regulated term in Canada, and any company can use it. At MattBed Canada we use it to describe high-density, extra-firm foam with a firm, flat feel. We do not claim it treats or corrects any condition.

  • Back and stomach sleepers — a firm surface means less sink, which many of these sleepers prefer
  • People used to a firm surface — if you grew up on a firm bed, memory foam may feel too soft at first
  • People who like a very firm surface — if you have ongoing back pain, ask a doctor or physiotherapist what firmness suits you; medium-firm did better than firm in one trial
  • Heavier sleepers — firm high-density foam supports higher body weight without sagging
  • Long-term durability — the double-sided construction means you flip it every 6 months and it lasts significantly longer

Which is Better for Back Pain Specifically?

The honest answer is that firmness matters more than mattress type, and it is personal. A 2003 randomized trial in The Lancet (313 adults with chronic non-specific low-back pain) found that a medium-firm mattress did better than a firm one on pain in bed and pain-related disability after 90 days. It did not test extra firm, and a mattress is not a treatment. See Firm vs Medium-Firm Mattress for Back Pain.

✅ If you prefer a hard surface — consider orthopedic-style

If you sleep on your back or stomach and like a firm, flat surface, an orthopedic-style mattress like the DualFirm is worth a look. Firmer is not automatically better for back pain, so give any new mattress a couple of weeks before judging it.

✅ For pressure point pain — choose memory foam

If you sleep on your side and feel pressure at the hips, knees or shoulders, many people find memory foam more comfortable because it contours to the body.

What About Temperature — Canada's Cold Winters?

This is an important question for Canadian buyers. Memory foam traps body heat — which can feel uncomfortable in summer but actually comfortable in winter. Orthopedic firm foam does not trap heat and tends to sleep cooler year-round.

For Calgary and Edmonton where temperatures drop to -30°C in winter, many families actually prefer memory foam for winter use.
